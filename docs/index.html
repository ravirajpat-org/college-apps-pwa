Columbia Engineering Different kind of path

Combined Plan (3-2) · Fu Foundation School of Engineering and Applied Science, New York · BA + BS, 5 years total

This one is not a high-school application target. She'd apply as a freshman to one of ~100 affiliate liberal-arts partner colleges (e.g. Middlebury, Goucher, Fordham), spend 3 years there, then apply into Columbia Engineering during the winter of her junior year of college. The actual Columbia admission event happens two years after high school, not during it.

Structure3 years at the partner college (BA coursework) + 2 years at Columbia Engineering (BS) = two degrees, 5 years total
Prerequisite courseworkFull Calculus sequence (through multivariable/Calc III–IV), 2–3 semesters Physics, General Chemistry + one lab, one Computer Science course, Intro Economics, ~27 nontechnical credits — completed at the partner college
GPA to transfer~3.0+ minimum overall and in pre-engineering coursework (exact bar varies by partner school — confirm with the specific affiliate)
ApplicationSubmitted via Columbia's application in winter of junior year of college · opens December, preferred deadline mid-Feb, final mid-March, decisions mid-April, fall entry only

Prep plan: the real near-term decision isn't about Columbia at all — it's picking a strong affiliate partner college as her actual senior-year application target, the same way she'd apply to any other school · once enrolled, front-load Calculus/Physics/Chemistry/CS in the first 3 years to hit the prerequisite list above · keep GPA comfortably above 3.0, ideally 3.3+, since pre-engineering coursework GPA is weighted separately and scrutinized · get the exact affiliate list and partner-specific terms from Columbia Engineering's Combined Plan office once a shortlist of colleges forms.

TU Delft Jan 15 fixed deadline

BSc Computer Science & Engineering · Delft, Netherlands · 3 years · Numerus fixus (590 seats/yr, 345 English track)

DiplomaAccredited US High School Diploma (ASCWASC, Cognia, MSA-CESS, NEASC, CIS, NCPSA, or state approval)
AP examsMinimum 4, each scored 3+, must include AP Calculus AB or BC
English testTOEFL iBT 90+ or IELTS 6.5+ — confirm if a US-schooling waiver applies
SAT/ACTNot used at all
GradesNOT used in ranking — admission is decided entirely by the test below + a lottery draw

Selection test (Cognitive Skills Test): two parts, each 50% — Mathematics (1 hr, Dutch VWO level: functions, differential/integral calculus, trig, geometry, vectors) and Systematic Reasoning & Logical Thinking (45 min, propositional/predicate logic, Boolean algebra). No calculator. Online proctored or on-campus. Score places her in one of 3 lottery categories per track; final rank is a random draw within that category.

Prep plan: AP Calculus BC on schedule · self-study discrete math/formal logic starting junior year — not covered by typical US coursework · use TU Delft's free pre-university calculus course + free "Delftse Foundations of Computation" ch.2 textbook, ~15–25 hrs recommended prep · confirm high school's accreditation body for the application file · register via Studielink from October senior year, hard deadline Jan 15, results Apr 15, starts September (no gap after June graduation).

KAIST Fully funded

Undergraduate, Computer Science track · Daejeon, South Korea · 4 years · Holistic admission

ApplicationTranscripts, personal statement, study plan/essay, up to 2 recommendation letters (optional but recommended)
SAT/ACT/APTechnically optional, effectively expected given competitiveness
English testTOEFL iBT 83 / IELTS 6.5+ — likely waived for US English-medium schooling with documentation
InterviewOnly if specifically requested by KAIST — not standard
DeadlinesEarly Admission: docs due ~Oct 22 · Regular Admission: ~Jan 14

Cost: all international undergrads get a full tuition waiver (up to 8 semesters) plus a ~$265–300/month stipend and health insurance, contingent on maintaining a GPA of just 2.7/4.3. Effectively $0 tuition — living costs (~$8,000–10,000/yr) are partly offset by the stipend.

Prep plan: strong SAT/ACT and/or AP scores (Calc BC, CS A, Physics) — treat as required despite being labeled optional · start drafting the personal statement/study plan early, since it carries real weight here unlike Monash/TU Delft · line up 1–2 strong recommendation letters, starting relationship-building junior year · confirm the English-test waiver with her school · track both the Early (Oct) and Regular (Jan) deadlines — Early is worth targeting given how competitive Regular gets.

UConn International Engineering Program Apply as UConn freshman

5-year dual degree: BA in a language/culture + BS in Engineering · University of Connecticut, Storrs

The accessible option here — there's no separate admission event. She'd apply to UConn as a normal incoming freshman (a far lower bar than Columbia or École Polytechnique), then opt into this program starting day one of college.

Tracks & partnersEurotech (Germany, Baden-Württemberg, 25+ years running) · Technopole (France, University of Toulouse, incl. Airbus internships) · ESP (Spain, Comillas Pontificia Madrid & Polytechnic University of Valencia) · AsiaTech (China, Shanghai)
CS relevanceAsiaTech (China) explicitly lists Computer Science as an engineering specialization — the clearest fit for her. UConn's Computer Science/Computer Engineering BS majors exist program-wide; combining them with Eurotech or Technopole specifically should be confirmed with the program office.
Language prerequisiteNone — true beginners start from scratch (e.g. German 1001); students with 3+ years of HS language start more advanced
StructureOne full year abroad in total immersion, typically between junior and senior year
Admission bar / deadlinesNot published online — GPA and program-specific deadlines require contacting the program office directly (International Engineering Program, 191 Auditorium Road, Unit 3187, Storrs, CT 06269)

Prep plan: the real admission event is just applying to UConn as a regular freshman — no extra reach-school prep needed for entry · declare interest in the International Engineering Program (ideally AsiaTech, for the direct CS tie-in) as early as possible, day one if she can · no need to front-load language study in high school, since true beginners are supported · contact the program office directly once she's shortlisting UConn to confirm current GPA/retention requirements and whether AsiaTech pairs cleanly with a Computer Science major.
